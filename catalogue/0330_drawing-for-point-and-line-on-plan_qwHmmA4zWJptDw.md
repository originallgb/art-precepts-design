## Precept Critique
This work systematically deconstructs visual primitives, offering a foundational lexicon for graphic design and interface development. Its rigorous isolation of each geometric form demonstrates a modular approach to visual composition, emphasizing clarity and analytical precision. The didactic presentation of these elements underscores the importance of understanding basic visual grammar as a prerequisite for complex visual communication systems.

---

## Actionable Design Heuristics
- Modular Component Design: Isolate and define fundamental visual components before combining them into complex structures.
- High-Contrast Legibility: Ensure primary information (figures) has maximum contrast against its background for immediate recognition.
- Hierarchical Annotation: Use consistent, subordinate visual cues (e.g., smaller, lighter text) for metadata or instructional labels to avoid competing with primary content.
- Grid-Based Organization: Employ an underlying grid to establish order, facilitate comparison, and provide visual breathing room between distinct elements.
- Progressive Disclosure of Complexity: Present simple, foundational forms first, then introduce variations or combinations to build understanding incrementally.
