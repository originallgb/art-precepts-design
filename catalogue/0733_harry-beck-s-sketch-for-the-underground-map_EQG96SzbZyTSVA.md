## Precept Critique
This artifact exemplifies the critical design principle of topological abstraction, demonstrating how spatial complexity can be rendered comprehensible by prioritizing relational clarity over geographical accuracy. Its visible iterative process, marked by layered lines and corrections, underscores the value of rapid prototyping in refining information architecture for user legibility. The consistent application of color-coding and uniform nodal representation establishes a robust visual grammar essential for navigating complex systems.

## Actionable Design Heuristics
- Prioritize topological accuracy over geographical precision when designing network diagrams to enhance user comprehension of connections and flow.
- Employ consistent visual encoding (e.g., distinct colors for different lines, uniform symbols for nodes) to create immediate recognition and reduce cognitive load in complex systems.
- Utilize an underlying, even if implied, orthogonal grid to provide structural coherence and simplify alignment for network elements, improving overall visual organization.
- Embrace iterative sketching and visible layering in the design process to facilitate rapid prototyping, exploration of alternatives, and refinement of information architecture.
- Design for clarity at intersections and decision points, ensuring that nodal representations are unambiguous and support efficient route selection.
