## Precept Critique
This work systematically demonstrates the principle of figure-ground reversal and the equivalence of positive and negative space within a constrained geometric system. It provides a foundational visual grammar for modular design, illustrating how simple orthogonal elements can generate complex yet balanced compositions through inversion and rearrangement. The piece functions as a didactic tool, dissecting visual perception to highlight the relational dynamics between forms rather than their individual identities.

## The 5 Evaluative Angles

### 1. Composition & Lineage
- **Focal Flow**: The eye is initially drawn to the two large square compositions, then sequentially analyzes the internal rectangular and linear elements within each, moving from left to right to compare the inverted figure-ground relationships.
- **Geometric Structure**: The composition is structured around a bipartite horizontal division of the canvas, framing two distinct but proportionally related square modules. Within each module, an implied orthogonal grid dictates the precise placement and alignment of the constituent rectangular and linear forms.
- **Symmetry & Balance**: The overall composition exhibits horizontal balance due to the symmetrical placement of the two primary square modules. Internally, each square achieves asymmetrical balance through the careful distribution of positive and negative space, where the visual weight of the forms is counteracted by their inverse counterparts.
