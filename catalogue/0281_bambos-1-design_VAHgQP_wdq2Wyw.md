## Precept Critique
This drawing rigorously articulates a modular architectural system through a stark, high-contrast graphic language, demonstrating how minimal elements can define complex spatial relationships. The systematic repetition and variation of cubic volumes establish a clear visual rhythm and structural logic, emphasizing industrial prefabrication and functional efficiency. Its precise rendering of form and void provides a foundational lesson in communicating architectural intent with absolute clarity and an economy of means.

## Actionable Design Heuristics
- Modular System Design: Employ a consistent, repeatable unit to construct complex forms, allowing for scalability and efficient production across various applications.
- High-Contrast Information Layering: Utilize stark binary contrasts (e.g., black and white) to differentiate primary functional elements (windows/voids) from structural components (walls/mass), ensuring immediate legibility and hierarchy.
- Elevated Base for Contextual Separation: Lift primary structures or interface elements from the ground plane to define a distinct functional zone, minimize perceived clutter, and create visual lightness.
- Grid-Based Composition for Structural Clarity: Organize elements along an underlying orthogonal grid to convey order, stability, and the potential for systematic expansion or modification in any design system.
- Economy of Line for Form Definition: Use minimal, precise lines to delineate forms and textures, prioritizing conceptual clarity and functional communication over decorative embellishment.
