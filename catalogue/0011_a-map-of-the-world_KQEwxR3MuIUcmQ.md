## Precept Critique
This medieval diagram exemplifies the power of hierarchical framing and color-coding to segment complex information into digestible, distinct zones. Its use of a limited, high-contrast palette, augmented by reflective gold, effectively establishes visual priority and enhances legibility on a textured substrate. The composition demonstrates how abstract geometric forms, when consistently applied, can create a robust, didactic visual system for conveying conceptual models.

## Actionable Design Heuristics
- Hierarchical Framing for Information Segmentation: Employ nested geometric containers (e.g., square within a page, circle within a square, bands within a circle) to clearly delineate distinct information blocks and establish a reading order.
- High-Contrast Outlines for Visual Priority: Utilize strong, contrasting borders (e.g., gold on blue, black on color) to define key elements and guide the eye, especially in complex or layered compositions.
- Limited, Purposeful Color Palette for Clarity: Restrict the color palette to a few distinct hues, assigning specific meanings or functions to each, to reduce cognitive load and reinforce categorical distinctions.
- Integrated Text for Contextual Labeling: Embed textual labels directly within the visual elements they describe, using a consistent, legible script, to provide immediate context without disrupting the overall visual flow.
- Leverage Materiality for Emphasis: Incorporate materials with distinct physical properties (e.g., reflective gold leaf against matte paint) to add tactile and visual richness, drawing attention to critical components.
