## Precept Critique
This work systematically explores the combinatorial potential of fundamental geometric forms and primary colors, establishing a visual lexicon for abstract representation. It functions as a pedagogical diagram, demonstrating principles of balance, hierarchy, and modularity through iterative variations. The precise articulation of each 'figure' underscores a rigorous analytical approach to visual grammar, offering a blueprint for constructing complex entities from elemental components.

## Actionable Design Heuristics
- Modular Composition: Design systems using a finite set of fundamental geometric primitives that can be combined and reconfigured to create diverse forms.
- Systematic Variation: Explore design possibilities by systematically altering parameters (color, orientation, stacking order) of core modules to generate a family of related but distinct entities.
- Visual Grammar for Abstraction: Establish a clear, consistent visual language where simple shapes and colors carry specific, repeatable structural or semantic roles.
- Hierarchical Stacking: Utilize vertical arrangement and size differentiation of geometric elements to imply relationships, stability, or progression within a composite form.
- Annotative Clarity: Integrate functional annotations directly into the visual field to provide context, instruction, or data, enhancing the utility of the visual information.
