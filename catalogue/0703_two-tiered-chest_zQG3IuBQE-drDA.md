## Precept Critique
This artifact demonstrates the principle of functional ornamentation, where structural necessities like corner brackets and hinges are elevated to primary aesthetic components. Its modular, stacked form illustrates efficient spatial organization and adaptability, a core tenet for scalable design systems. The deliberate contrast between the dark, organic wood and the bright, geometric brass highlights material interplay as a fundamental design driver.

---

## Actionable Design Heuristics
- Functional Ornamentation: Integrate structural or operational components (e.g., buttons, connectors, chassis elements) into the primary aesthetic language, making them visually prominent and contributing to the overall design identity.
- Modular Stacking Principle: Design systems or products as discrete, stackable units that can be combined to create larger, more complex configurations, enhancing scalability and user customization.
- Material Contrast for Hierarchy: Utilize distinct material properties (e.g., matte vs. glossy, dark vs. light, organic vs. metallic) to establish visual hierarchy and guide user interaction with key elements.
- Repetitive Element Reinforcement: Employ consistent repetition of geometric forms and hardware elements to establish visual rhythm, reinforce structural integrity, and create a sense of order and completeness.
- Exposed Joinery as Feature: Treat joinery and fastening mechanisms not merely as functional necessities but as visible, intentional design features that communicate craftsmanship and durability.
