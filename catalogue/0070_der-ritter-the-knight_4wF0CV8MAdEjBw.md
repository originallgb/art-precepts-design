## The 5 Evaluative Angles

### 1. Composition & Lineage
- **Focal Flow**: The eye is drawn from the upper left, following the knight's sword and aggressive posture, diagonally downward to the right, where Death's counter-thrust and the fallen hourglass create a point of tension and resolution. The knight's gaze directs attention towards Death's skeletal form.
- **Geometric Structure**: The composition is dominated by a strong diagonal axis running from the upper left to the lower right, established by the sword and the opposing stances of the figures. A secondary horizontal line defines the distant landscape, grounding the dynamic action within a shallow pictorial space. The overall image is contained within a thick, rectilinear frame.
- **Symmetry & Balance**: Asymmetrical balance, with the larger, more detailed knight on the left counterbalanced by the dynamic, forward-leaning skeleton on the right. The visual weight is distributed through active poses and implied motion rather than static equilibrium.
- **Framing Density**: dense
- **Historical Lineage**: German Renaissance woodcut, Dance of Death iconography, Northern European graphic arts (16th century)
