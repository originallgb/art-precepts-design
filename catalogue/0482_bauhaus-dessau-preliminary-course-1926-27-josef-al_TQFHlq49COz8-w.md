## Precept Critique
This work rigorously demonstrates the generation of perceived volume and spatial relationships through the systematic application of planar hatching patterns. It functions as a didactic exploration of how linear density and orientation can simulate curvature and depth on a two-dimensional surface. The piece underscores the Bauhaus principle of material and form exploration, translating abstract geometric concepts into tangible visual instruction.

---

## Actionable Design Heuristics
- Utilize systematic line density and orientation (hatching) to convey three-dimensional form and curvature on a 2D interface, providing depth cues without complex shading.
- Deconstruct complex volumetric objects into simpler, intersecting planar components, then represent these components individually to illustrate their assembly and structural logic.
- Employ consistent visual rules, such as specific line angles for different planes, to create a legible and predictable system for interpreting spatial relationships and material properties in abstract diagrams.
- Leverage abstraction to represent complex objects through their fundamental geometric primitives and their interactions, enabling users to grasp underlying structural principles without visual clutter.
